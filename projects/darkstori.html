01 — THE QUESTION

What if the operations tools could reason across the whole store?

A forecast matters more when it can influence a decision someone has to make today.

Dark stores operate within tight constraints: short delivery windows, small footprints, changing demand, limited picking capacity, and perishable inventory. Planning each piece in a separate tool makes it hard to see how one decision affects another.

I shaped DarkStori as an operations surface that connects network planning with what happens inside and around a store after orders arrive.

02 — THE SYSTEM

Move from a neighborhood signal to an operator action.

The product brings together location scoring, demand prediction, store slotting, dispatch batching, and inventory decisions. Different stages use different inputs and algorithms, while the API gives the interface a shared place to work from.

03 — ENGINEERING CHOICES

Match the algorithm to the decision being made.

01

Keep location scores explainable

Candidate ranking exposes weighted factors for predicted demand, competitor gap, demographics, and road distance. A score can be decomposed instead of surfaced as a mystery number.

↗
02

Use forecast context beyond the calendar

The prediction service looks up historical lagged orders and incorporates date, holiday, and weather services when preparing demand estimates.

↗
03

Optimize dispatch as a route problem

The batch optimizer estimates travel distance, builds rider batches with a Clarke–Wright savings heuristic, and applies 2-Opt tour improvement. Those are modeled route estimates, not a guarantee of live delivery times.

↗
04

Put fast-moving items near the work

The slotting engine models shelf coordinates relative to the staging bay and uses product affinity and velocity to propose store layouts.

↗

04 — WHAT I’M TAKING FORWARD

Optimization needs an operator on the other side.

A strong recommendation is only useful if a person can understand it, act on it, and correct it.

DarkStori has helped me think about software as a chain of operational decisions instead of a collection of dashboards. The hard product work is connecting the numbers to a choice without hiding the assumptions behind them.

The next design step is stronger decision provenance: show which inputs drove a recommendation, collect the operator’s outcome, and use that feedback to improve the next forecast or plan.
