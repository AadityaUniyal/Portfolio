01 — THE QUESTION

How can separate health features feel like one useful routine?

Wellness apps ask people to repeat small actions. The product has to make each action feel connected to the next.

Logging a meal, planning a workout, checking recovery, and reviewing progress are related—but they often live in separate screens and tools. That separation can make it hard to use personal context consistently.

Fitness Smarty explores a joined experience: capture information quickly, use it in planning, and let the person review or adjust what the system suggests.

02 — THE SYSTEM

Capture, calculate, recommend, and bring the result back.

A React and TypeScript client connects to a FastAPI backend with user, meal, nutrition, workout, and progress services. The repository contains Gemini-based meal scanning, a detector abstraction with optional YOLO integration, and rule / history-based recommendation modules.

03 — ENGINEERING CHOICES

Keep recommendations personal—and inspectable.

01

Make image scanning asynchronous

The meal scan route validates the uploaded image, returns a task ID, and queues background analysis. A long-running model interaction does not have to hold the initial request open.

↗
02

Separate recognition from nutrition math

The meal analysis service composes image processing, food detection, nutrition lookup, portion estimates, and nutrition totals as distinct steps. The detector supports optional YOLO loading; its documented mock fallback should not be confused with a universally active trained model.

↗
03

Blend rules and generated coaching

The unified coach combines recommendation and planning components, recovery calculations, and a hybrid ranker, with Gemini used for the coaching narrative. Advice remains something a user can review, not a clinical diagnosis.

↗
04

Account for fatigue across sessions

The recovery engine models muscle groups and their relationships, applies time-based fatigue decay, and gates exercise recommendations based on accumulated load.

↗

04 — WHAT I’M TAKING FORWARD

Personalization needs clarity about where the suggestion came from.

A recommendation feels personal when it uses context, but also when it gives the person room to disagree.

Fitness Smarty has been a broad end-to-end exercise in connecting a user interface to model-powered features, domain services, and persistent progress. It has also made me think about how to build trust into wellness products.

The next step is to make each suggestion explain its inputs: the meal estimate, training history, recovery signal, and goal that shaped it. Then the user can correct the context instead of accepting an opaque answer.
